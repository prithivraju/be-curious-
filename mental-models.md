# Mental Models Skill

Apply powerful thinking frameworks from **The Great Mental Models** by Shane Parrish (Farnam Street) to any problem or decision.

## When to Use This Skill
- Analyzing complex problems
- Making important decisions
- Evaluating options and trade-offs
- Understanding systems and relationships
- Breaking through thinking blocks
- Explaining concepts clearly

---

## Volume 1: General Thinking Concepts

### 1. The Map Is Not the Territory
**What it is:** Our representations of reality (maps, models, descriptions) are NOT reality itself.

**How to apply:**
- Question your assumptions about what you think you know
- Remember models have limitations and may be outdated
- Look for what's missing from your current understanding

**Example:** A company org chart shows reporting lines but not actual influence, relationships, or how work really gets done.

---

### 2. Circle of Competence
**What it is:** Know what you understand well and stay within that domain for important decisions.

**How to apply:**
- Identify your true areas of expertise
- Be honest about what you DON'T know
- Seek experts when operating outside your circle
- Expand your circle deliberately over time

**Example:** Warren Buffett only invests in businesses he deeply understands, avoiding tech stocks for decades.

---

### 3. First Principles Thinking
**What it is:** Break problems down to fundamental truths, then rebuild from there.

**How to apply:**
1. Identify assumptions in the current approach
2. Ask "Why?" repeatedly until you hit bedrock truths
3. Build up a solution from those fundamentals

**Example:** Elon Musk on batteries: Instead of accepting "batteries are expensive," he asked what raw materials cost and found batteries could be much cheaper.

---

### 4. Thought Experiment
**What it is:** Imagine scenarios to test ideas without real-world constraints.

**How to apply:**
- Ask "What if...?" questions
- Mentally simulate outcomes
- Test edge cases in your mind
- Challenge assumptions safely

**Example:** Einstein imagined riding alongside a beam of light, leading to special relativity.

---

### 5. Second-Order Thinking
**What it is:** Think beyond immediate consequences to long-term and indirect effects.

**How to apply:**
- Ask "And then what?" after identifying first-order effects
- Consider how others will react to your actions
- Map out chains of consequences
- Look for unintended consequences

**Example:** Rent control (1st order: lower rents) → (2nd order: less housing built, landlords neglect properties, housing shortage worsens)

---

### 6. Probabilistic Thinking
**What it is:** Think in terms of likelihoods rather than certainties.

**How to apply:**
- Assign rough probabilities to outcomes
- Update beliefs as new information arrives
- Consider multiple possible futures
- Avoid overconfidence in single predictions

**Example:** Instead of "This will work," think "There's a 70% chance this works, 20% partial success, 10% failure."

---

### 7. Inversion
**What it is:** Approach problems backwards—instead of seeking success, avoid failure.

**How to apply:**
- Ask "What would guarantee failure?" then avoid those things
- Think about what you DON'T want
- Identify obstacles by working backwards from the goal

**Example:** To have a good marriage, ask "What would ruin a marriage?" (neglect, dishonesty, contempt) and avoid those.

---

### 8. Occam's Razor
**What it is:** The simplest explanation is usually correct.

**How to apply:**
- Don't multiply assumptions unnecessarily
- Prefer simpler theories over complex ones
- Be skeptical of elaborate explanations

**Example:** If your car won't start, check the battery before assuming the engine failed.

---

### 9. Hanlon's Razor
**What it is:** Never attribute to malice what can be explained by incompetence, ignorance, or accident.

**How to apply:**
- Assume good intent first
- Consider simpler explanations for bad behavior
- Don't create enemies where none exist

**Example:** Your colleague didn't respond to your email probably because they're busy, not because they hate you.

## Volume 2: Physics, Chemistry, and Biology

### Physics Models

#### Leverage
Small force applied at the right point moves mountains.
- **Apply:** Find high-leverage activities that produce outsized results

#### Inertia
Objects resist change in motion.
- **Apply:** Understand why change is hard; initial push requires more energy

#### Velocity
Direction matters as much as speed.
- **Apply:** Working hard in the wrong direction is worse than working slowly in the right one

#### Friction
Resistance slows everything down.
- **Apply:** Reduce friction in processes to increase flow

### Chemistry Models

#### Activation Energy
Minimum energy needed to start a reaction.
- **Apply:** Front-load effort to kickstart habits/projects; once started, momentum helps

#### Catalysts
Substances that speed up reactions without being consumed.
- **Apply:** Be a catalyst for change; find catalysts that accelerate your goals

#### Alloying
Combining elements creates new properties.
- **Apply:** Mix skills, ideas, or people to create something stronger than individual parts

### Biology Models

#### Ecosystems
Everything is connected in webs of relationships.
- **Apply:** Consider ripple effects; understand the whole system, not just parts

#### Natural Selection
What works survives; what doesn't, dies.
- **Apply:** Test ideas, keep what works, discard what doesn't

#### Niches
Specialized roles reduce competition.
- **Apply:** Find your unique niche rather than competing head-to-head

#### Cooperation
Working together often beats competition.
- **Apply:** Look for win-win arrangements

## Volume 3: Systems and Mathematics

### Systems Models

#### Feedback Loops
Small changes amplify (positive) or stabilize (negative).
- **Apply:** Identify feedback loops; reinforce positive ones, break negative ones

#### Equilibrium
Systems tend toward balance points.
- **Apply:** Understand stable states; know that pushing away from equilibrium takes energy

#### Bottlenecks
The narrowest point limits the whole system.
- **Apply:** Find and fix bottlenecks; don't optimize non-bottleneck areas

#### Margin of Safety
Build systems to handle more than expected.
- **Apply:** Always leave buffer room for the unexpected

#### Scale
Properties change as things get bigger or smaller.
- **Apply:** What works at small scale may not work at large scale

#### Critical Mass
The tipping point where change becomes self-sustaining.
- **Apply:** Push to critical mass; after that, momentum takes over

### Mathematics Models

#### Compounding
Small gains accumulate into massive results over time.
- **Apply:** Invest early, be patient, let time work for you

#### Law of Diminishing Returns
Each additional unit produces less value.
- **Apply:** Know when to stop optimizing and move to the next thing

#### Regression to the Mean
Extreme results tend to move back toward average.
- **Apply:** Don't overreact to outliers; expect normalization

## Volume 4: Economics and Art

### Economics Models

#### Scarcity
Limited resources force choices.
- **Apply:** Prioritize ruthlessly; you can't have everything

#### Supply and Demand
Price emerges from the interaction of availability and desire.
- **Apply:** Understand market dynamics; scarcity increases value

#### Trade-offs
Every choice has a cost (what you gave up).
- **Apply:** Make trade-offs explicit; compare opportunity costs

#### Specialization
Focus enables mastery and efficiency.
- **Apply:** Specialize in your strengths; trade for what others do better

#### Creative Destruction
Old systems must die for new ones to emerge.
- **Apply:** Embrace necessary endings; destruction enables creation

#### Debt
Borrowing from the future has hidden costs.
- **Apply:** Be wary of technical debt, sleep debt, relationship debt

### Art Models

#### Framing
Context shapes perception.
- **Apply:** How you present something changes how it's received

#### Storytelling
Narratives are how humans understand the world.
- **Apply:** Use stories to communicate ideas; facts alone don't persuade

#### Contrast
Differences create emphasis and clarity.
- **Apply:** Place things in contrast to highlight importance

#### Audience
Every communication has an intended receiver.
- **Apply:** Know your audience; adapt your message accordingly

## How to Use Mental Models

### The Mental Models Approach

1. **Identify the problem or decision**
2. **Select relevant models** from the list above
3. **Apply multiple lenses** - use 2-3 models minimum
4. **Look for convergence** - where do different models agree?
5. **Check for blind spots** - what are you missing?
6. **Decide and act** with greater clarity

### Quick Reference: Model Selection by Situation

| Situation | Recommended Models |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Making a big decision** | Inversion, Second-Order Thinking, Probabilistic Thinking |
| **Solving a complex problem** | First Principles, Ecosystems, Systems Thinking |
| **Starting something new** | Activation Energy, Circle of Competence, Margin of Safety |
| **Understanding why change is hard** | Inertia, Equilibrium, Feedback Loops |
| **Evaluating an opportunity** | Supply/Demand, Trade-offs, Opportunity Cost |
| **Communicating ideas** | Storytelling, Framing, Audience |
| **Building something sustainable** | Compounding, Niches, Cooperation |

## Examples

**Input:** "Should I take this new job offer?"

**Output using mental models:**

Let me analyze this using several mental models:

1. **Inversion:** What would make this job terrible? (bad boss, no growth, wrong direction)
2. **Second-Order Thinking:** If I take it, then what? (new skills → more opportunities → ?)
3. **Circle of Competence:** Does this leverage my strengths or push me outside dangerously?
4. **Opportunity Cost:** What do I give up by saying yes?
5. **Compounding:** Which path compounds better over 5-10 years?

The models converge on: [analysis based on applying these lenses]
